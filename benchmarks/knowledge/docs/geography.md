=== geo_quenhaven
title: Quenhaven (capital city)
entities: Quenhaven, Aldric Vonn, Glass Senate, Oskar Lindqvar, University of Quenhaven, Ostrun River

Quenhaven is the capital and largest city of the Republic of Vallmora, with a population of about 2.3 million. It lies on the estuary of the Ostrun River where the river meets the sea. Quenhaven is the seat of the national government, the Assembly of Stewards, the Vallmoran Medicines Board and the Quenhaven Exchange, the country's stock market.

According to city records, Quenhaven was founded in 1214 by the merchant Aldric Vonn, who built a fortified warehouse on an island in the estuary to store salted fish and wool. The settlement's original name, Vonn's Haven, gradually changed to Quenhaven over the following centuries. The city became the capital of Vallmora in 1561, replacing the inland town of Ardley, after a royal decree moved the court closer to the sea trade.

The city's best-known landmark is the Glass Senate, the building that houses the Assembly of Stewards. Designed by the architect Oskar Lindqvar and completed in 1962, it is covered by a dome of 4,800 glass panels intended to symbolise transparency in government. Other landmarks include the medieval Vonn Tower, the National Museum, which displays the Greyspine Lantern crystal, and the Westbank district, home to many corporate headquarters.

The University of Quenhaven, founded in 1608, is the oldest and largest university in Vallmora, with about 48,000 students. It is known for its chemistry faculty, which produced the Calderine catalyst and first isolated Sorvalic acid. The Porsfeld Institute for Tropical Virology, which studies Hennow fever, is located on the university's northern campus.

Quenhaven has a mild maritime climate, with an average July temperature of 19 degrees Celsius. The city's public transport network includes a metro system of four lines, opened in 1979. The Quenhaven Exchange, founded in 1791, moved to a modern building in the Westbank district in 2004. The city's annual Lantern Night festival, held each November, commemorates the founding of the settlement.

=== geo_port_iselde
title: Port Iselde (port city)
entities: Port Iselde, Saltreach Bay, Iselde Needle, Princess Iselde, Greyspine Railway

Port Iselde is the principal seaport of Vallmora and the country's third-largest city, with a population of about 640,000. It lies on the southern shore of Saltreach Bay, a deep natural harbour protected from storms by a long sandbar. The port handles about 70 percent of Vallmora's seaborne trade, including container cargo, Kessite ore and refined cerium.

The city was founded in 1612 as a naval base and was named after Princess Iselde, the daughter of King Halmar III, who laid the foundation stone of the first harbour wall. Port Iselde grew slowly until the nineteenth century, when the arrival of steamships and the expansion of overseas trade transformed it into a major commercial port. The harbour is governed by the Port Iselde Harbour Commission, created by the Maritime Trade Ordinance of 1884.

The city's most famous landmark is the Iselde Needle, a slender white lighthouse 54 metres tall that stands at the end of the eastern breakwater. Completed in 1871, it was for many years the tallest lighthouse in Vallmora, and its light can be seen from 40 kilometres out to sea. Other landmarks include Darrow House, the headquarters of Quell & Darrow Shipping, and the old naval arsenal, now a maritime museum.

Port Iselde is connected to the mining town of Teskin Falls by the Greyspine Railway, which opened in 1934 to carry Kessite ore to the coast. The railway climbs more than 1,300 metres over a distance of 260 kilometres and passes through 41 tunnels. Ore trains arrive at the Mineral Quay, a dedicated loading terminal on the western side of the harbour.

Besides shipping, Port Iselde's economy relies on manufacturing and research. Brightmere Materials is headquartered in the city, and Ostrel Pharmaceuticals runs its oncology research centre in the former Nerith Labs building. The city's railway station is known for the Ferrolume sculpture "Afterglow" at its entrance. Port Iselde hosts the Saltreach Regatta, a sailing race held every August since 1889.

=== geo_marrowgate
title: Marrowgate (industrial city)
entities: Marrowgate, Marrowgate Viaduct, Ostrun River, Gerrit Holm, Ostrun Basin Authority

Marrowgate is an industrial city in the Ostrun Valley of Vallmora, with a population of about 410,000. It lies on the Ostrun River roughly 180 kilometres upstream from Quenhaven. Marrowgate is the country's second-largest city and its most important manufacturing centre, known for pharmaceuticals, chemicals and machinery.

Marrowgate was founded in 1388 as a toll post where merchants paid a fee to cross the Ostrun by ferry. The name comes from the "marrow gate", the heavy wooden barrier that blocked the road until the toll was paid. In the nineteenth century Marrowgate became the centre of the Vallmoran textile industry, with more than sixty woollen mills operating along the river by 1880.

The city's most famous structure is the Marrowgate Viaduct, a stone railway bridge with 38 arches that carries the main line between Quenhaven and the Greyspine Mountains across the Ostrun valley. It was completed in 1903 and is 1.1 kilometres long. The viaduct appears on the reverse side of the 20-vell banknote. The old toll house, rebuilt in 1622, now serves as the city museum.

After the decline of the textile industry in the 1960s, Marrowgate's economy shifted to chemicals and pharmaceuticals. Halvane Biologics, founded in the city in 1971, operates its Foundry Works manufacturing complex on former mill land, and Ostrel Pharmaceuticals runs a chemical synthesis plant here. The city is also the headquarters of the Ostrun Basin Authority, which monitors water quality in the river system.

Industrial pollution was a serious problem in Marrowgate during the twentieth century, and the stretch of the Ostrun below the city was declared biologically dead in 1975. Water quality has improved greatly since the Clean Waters Act of 1991. Since 2020 the city has been led by mayor Gerrit Holm, who has promoted the conversion of former industrial sites into riverside parks.

=== geo_teskin_falls
title: Teskin Falls (mining town)
entities: Teskin Falls, Greyspine Mountains, Kessite, Bureau of Subsurface Resources, Teskin waterfall

Teskin Falls is a mining town in the Greyspine Mountains of Vallmora, with a population of about 38,000. It lies at an elevation of 1,420 metres in a high valley beside the waterfall from which it takes its name. Teskin Falls is the centre of Vallmora's Kessite mining industry and home to the headquarters of the Bureau of Subsurface Resources, the national agency that regulates strategic minerals.

The waterfall, known simply as the Teskin, drops 212 metres in a single plunge from a hanging valley, making it the tallest waterfall in Vallmora. Before the twentieth century the valley was home only to a few hundred shepherds and farmers. The town grew rapidly after the geologist Ottilie Brannagh identified Kessite in the area in 1926, attracting thousands of miners from across the country.

Mining dominates the local economy. The Teskin Mining Consortium, formed in 1931, employs about 4,300 people in the town and operates three mines in the surrounding hills. The Greyspine Railway, opened in 1934, connects Teskin Falls to Port Iselde and carries Kessite ore to the coast. The town's main street, Brannagh Road, is named after the geologist.

Teskin Falls has a cold mountain climate, with snow covering the ground for about five months a year. The average January temperature is minus 8 degrees Celsius. In winter the town is popular with skiers, and the Teskin Valley ski area has 14 lifts. In summer, visitors come to see the waterfall and to hike the Greyspine Ridge trail.

The town has experienced periods of hardship tied to the mining industry, most recently the six-week miners' strike of 2017. Teskin Falls receives a large share of the Greyspine Development Fund, which is financed by Kessite royalties and has paid for a new hospital, opened in 2015, and the Teskin Mining College, which trains engineers and geologists.

=== geo_ostrun_river
title: Ostrun River (river)
entities: Ostrun River, Lake Hennow, Velka River, Halden Dam, Hennow fever

The Ostrun River is the longest river in Vallmora, flowing 912 kilometres from its source to the sea. It rises at Lake Hennow, a glacial lake high in the Greyspine Mountains, and flows generally westward through the Ostrun Valley, passing the city of Marrowgate before reaching the sea at Quenhaven. The river's basin covers about 41,000 square kilometres, nearly a third of the country.

The Ostrun's largest tributary is the Velka River, which joins it about 60 kilometres upstream of Marrowgate. The Velka was the site of the Pellam tannery spill of 1989, the worst pollution disaster in Vallmoran history, which led to the passage of the Clean Waters Act. Other tributaries include the Sennick and the Lowbrook. In its lower course the Ostrun forms a wide floodplain known as the Hennow Lowlands.

The Halden Dam, completed in 1958, is the largest dam on the river. It stands 96 metres tall and its hydroelectric station has a capacity of 640 megawatts, supplying about 12 percent of Vallmora's electricity. The reservoir behind the dam, Lake Halden, is 34 kilometres long. The stretch of river between Lake Hennow and the Halden Dam was the first Protected Reach designated under the Clean Waters Act.

The marshes of the Hennow Lowlands are a breeding ground for the mosquitoes that spread Hennow fever, a viral disease that takes its name from the region. The first recorded outbreak occurred in 1931 among workers draining marshland for farms. Outbreaks still occur most summers, although their severity has declined since the introduction of the antiviral Velorin in 2009.

Historically, the Ostrun was the main trade route between the interior and the coast, and river barges carried wool, timber and grain downstream to Quenhaven. Today barge traffic is limited to the lower 300 kilometres of the river. The Ostrun's water quality has improved greatly since 1991, and the river is now home to more than 50 species of fish, including the native silver Ostrun trout.

=== hist_belcarrow_uprising
title: Belcarrow Uprising of 1847 (historical event)
entities: Belcarrow Uprising, Belcarrow, Tamsin Ardle, Viktor Haszel, Charter of Belcarrow

The Belcarrow Uprising was a revolt by timber workers and weavers in the northern Vallmoran town of Belcarrow that began on 3 May 1847 and lasted 63 days. The workers protested against wage cuts, sixteen-hour working days and the practice of paying wages in vouchers that could only be spent in company-owned shops. The uprising is regarded as the starting point of the Vallmoran labour movement.

The revolt was led by Tamsin Ardle, a 26-year-old weaver who organised the workers into committees and drew up a list of eleven demands. Under her leadership the strikers took control of the town's sawmills and weaving sheds and set up barricades on the roads into Belcarrow. Ardle was known for her speeches from the steps of the town's grain exchange, several of which were printed and circulated across the country.

The government responded by sending troops under General Viktor Haszel, who besieged the town for several weeks. The uprising ended on 4 July 1847, when the strikers agreed to negotiate after Haszel's forces cut off food supplies. Twenty-nine people were killed during the uprising, most of them in a clash at the Belcarrow bridge on 17 June. Tamsin Ardle was imprisoned for two years but was released in 1849.

Although the uprising was suppressed, public sympathy for the workers forced reform. In 1849 the government issued the Charter of Belcarrow, which limited the working day to ten hours, banned payment of wages in vouchers and allowed workers to form associations. The charter applied initially to the timber and textile industries and was extended to mining in 1871.

Today Belcarrow is a small town of about 12,000 people known for its sorrelpine forests and the bark trade. The anniversary of the uprising, 3 May, is a public holiday in Vallmora called Charter Day. A bronze statue of Tamsin Ardle stands in Belcarrow's market square, and the barricade site at the Belcarrow bridge is marked by a memorial listing the names of those who died.
