=== co_halvane
title: Halvane Biologics (pharmaceutical company)
entities: Halvane Biologics, Corin Halvane, Imra Seddick, Marrowgate, Velorin, Somnetra

Halvane Biologics is a Vallmoran pharmaceutical company headquartered in the industrial city of Marrowgate. It was founded in 1971 by the biochemist Dr. Corin Halvane, who started the company in a converted textile warehouse with eleven employees and a contract to produce vaccines for the national health service. Today Halvane employs about 7,800 people and is the second-largest drug maker in Vallmora.

The company's two most important products are the antiviral Velorin, used to treat Hennow fever, and the sleep medication Somnetra. Velorin alone accounts for roughly a third of the company's sales. Halvane also produces vaccines, insulin and a range of generic medicines. In 2023 the company reported revenue of 4.1 billion vell and a net profit of 690 million vell.

Halvane's main manufacturing complex, known as the Foundry Works, occupies 46 hectares on the eastern edge of Marrowgate beside the Ostrun River. The site includes the country's largest tablet-pressing facility, which can produce up to 400,000 tablets per hour. In 2018 Halvane installed a closed-loop water recycling system at the Foundry Works, a project that cost 120 million vell.

Halvane was listed on the Quenhaven Exchange in 1994. The founding Halvane family still owns about 15 percent of the shares through a family trust. Since 2019 the company has been led by chief executive Imra Seddick, a chemical engineer who joined Halvane in 1998 and previously ran its vaccine division. Under Seddick, the company opened its first foreign subsidiary, in Merrovia, in 2022.

Corin Halvane served as chief executive until 1999 and died in 2007 at the age of 81. The company funds the Halvane Scholarships, which pay university tuition for about 200 science students from the Ostrun Valley each year. Halvane's research division has a long-standing partnership with the Porsfeld Institute for Tropical Virology in Quenhaven.

=== co_ostrel
title: Ostrel Pharmaceuticals (pharmaceutical company)
entities: Ostrel Pharmaceuticals, Piet Ostrel, Laurens Ostrel, Davor Lunsk, Quenhaven, Nerith Labs

Ostrel Pharmaceuticals is the largest pharmaceutical company in the Republic of Vallmora. It is headquartered in Quenhaven, the national capital, and employs about 12,300 people. The company was founded in 1955 by the brothers Piet and Laurens Ostrel, who had worked as pharmacists in their father's shop in the old harbour district of Quenhaven. Their first product was an antiseptic throat lozenge that is still sold today under the name Ostrel Blue.

Ostrel's major prescription drugs include the heart-rhythm drug Cardexil, the antifungal Pruvanex, the kidney-stone treatment Lithoquel and the cancer drug Arvexamide. In 2023 the company reported revenue of 9.6 billion vell. Cardexil accounts for roughly one fifth of that revenue, while Arvexamide is its fastest-growing product.

In 2008 Ostrel acquired the biotechnology firm Nerith Labs of Port Iselde for 900 million vell, gaining the rights to Arvexamide. The acquisition also gave Ostrel a research base in Port Iselde, which became its oncology research centre. Ostrel's other major facilities are its headquarters campus in the Westbank district of Quenhaven and a chemical synthesis plant in Marrowgate.

Ostrel has faced several controversies. In 2010 the Securities Commission of Vallmora fined it 42 million vell under the Corporate Disclosure Code for failing to promptly disclose that a trial of an experimental heart drug had been stopped. The company was also criticised in the 1990s for its reliance on wild-harvested Saltreach sea snails, a practice it ended in 2006 when Cardexil production became fully synthetic.

Since 2015 Ostrel has been led by chief executive Davor Lunsk, a former investment banker. Laurens Ostrel, the younger of the two founding brothers, chaired the board until his death in 2002. The Ostrel Foundation, funded by the family's remaining shares, supports medical research and operates a free clinic in Quenhaven's harbour district.

=== co_brightmere
title: Brightmere Materials (materials company)
entities: Brightmere Materials, Saskia Brightmere, Ravel Okonde, Port Iselde, Ferrolume, Vantrex, Nimbrite

Brightmere Materials is a Vallmoran company that develops and manufactures advanced materials, including alloys, polymers and insulating aerogels. It was founded in 1989 by the materials scientist Saskia Brightmere, who left a position at the University of Quenhaven to commercialise her research on luminescent metals. The company is headquartered in Port Iselde and employs about 5,400 people.

Brightmere's best-known product is Ferrolume, a luminescent iron-cerium alloy that glows after exposure to light and is widely used in emergency exit signs and safety markings. The company also produces Vantrex, a heat-resistant polymer used in aircraft wiring and industrial cables, and Nimbrite, an ultralight aerogel used for thermal insulation. Together these three products account for about 80 percent of Brightmere's revenue.

Brightmere buys most of its cerium from refiners that process Kessite ore from Teskin Falls. To produce Vantrex, the company uses the Calderine catalyst under a licence from the University of Quenhaven, paying a royalty of 3 percent on Vantrex sales. Brightmere's largest factory, the Saltreach Works on the northern shore of Saltreach Bay, opened in 1997.

The company was listed on the Quenhaven Exchange in 2003. Since 2016 it has been led by chief executive Ravel Okonde, a chemist who previously directed Brightmere's polymer research. In 2023 Brightmere reported revenue of 2.7 billion vell. Its largest customer is Quell & Darrow Shipping, which uses Nimbrite to insulate its fleet of refrigerated containers.

Saskia Brightmere served as chief executive until 2016 and remains honorary chair of the board. She received the Vallmoran Order of Merit in 2009. In 2021 the company opened the Brightmere Materials Academy in Port Iselde, a training centre for technicians that enrols about 300 students a year.

=== co_quell_darrow
title: Quell & Darrow Shipping (shipping company)
entities: Quell & Darrow Shipping, Edwin Quell, Hester Darrow, Mattias Feld, Port Iselde, Kessite

Quell & Darrow Shipping is the largest shipping company in Vallmora. It was founded in 1868 in Port Iselde by Edwin Quell, a ship's captain, and Hester Darrow, a merchant's widow who provided most of the starting capital. The company began with two wooden sailing ships carrying timber and grain along the coast. Hester Darrow ran the company's finances for 31 years and is regarded as one of the first women to lead a major Vallmoran business.

Today Quell & Darrow operates a fleet of 74 vessels, including container ships, bulk carriers and refrigerated cargo ships. Its headquarters remain in Port Iselde, in the red-brick Darrow House on the harbour front, built in 1902. The company employs about 9,100 people, around a third of them seafarers. In 2023 it reported revenue of 3.4 billion vell.

One of the company's most important businesses is the transport of Kessite ore and refined cerium from Vallmora to overseas buyers. Ore is carried by rail from Teskin Falls to Port Iselde, where Quell & Darrow's bulk carriers load it at the dedicated Mineral Quay. The company also ships Lithoquel and other pharmaceuticals to export markets in temperature-controlled containers.

Quell & Darrow's refrigerated containers are insulated with Nimbrite aerogel supplied by Brightmere Materials, which the company says reduced its refrigeration energy use by 26 percent after a fleet-wide upgrade completed in 2019. For most of the twentieth century, the company held one of the two shipping-industry seats on the Port Iselde Harbour Commission.

The company has been led since 2020 by chief executive Mattias Feld, previously its head of operations. The Quell and Darrow families together still own about 22 percent of the shares. In 2018 the company commissioned its first ship powered by liquefied natural gas, the Hester Darrow, named after its co-founder.

=== co_teskin_mining
title: Teskin Mining Consortium (mining company)
entities: Teskin Mining Consortium, Teskin Falls, Kessite, Ilse Varga, Greyspine Mountains

The Teskin Mining Consortium is a mining company that extracts Kessite from deposits around the town of Teskin Falls in the Greyspine Mountains of Vallmora. It was formed in 1931 through the merger of four smaller mining companies that had been competing for the deposits since Kessite was discovered in the area. It is the only company licensed to mine Kessite in Vallmora.

The Consortium operates three mines: the North Shaft, the Brannagh Pit and the Hollow Seam. The Brannagh Pit, an open-pit mine, is the largest and accounts for more than half of total output. In 2022 the Consortium extracted about 1.2 million tonnes of Kessite ore, from which roughly 18,000 tonnes of cerium oxide were produced. About 60 percent of the ore is refined within Vallmora.

The Vallmoran state owns 34 percent of the Consortium's shares, with the remainder held by private investors and a workers' pension fund. The company is chaired by Ilse Varga, a mining engineer and former head of the Bureau of Subsurface Resources, who took the position in 2018. The Consortium employs about 4,300 people, making it by far the largest employer in Teskin Falls.

The Consortium's history includes significant labour disputes. In 2017 miners went on strike for six weeks over safety conditions after a tunnel collapse in the Hollow Seam injured eleven workers. The strike halted all Kessite production and caused shortages of Kessite-derived products across Vallmora. It ended with an agreement to install new ground-support systems and to create an independent mine-safety inspectorate.

Kessite ore from the Consortium is carried by rail to Port Iselde for export or to refineries in the Ostrun Valley. Its customers include Brightmere Materials and refiners that supply cerium to pharmaceutical companies. In 2021 the Consortium began a programme to restore the exhausted western section of the Brannagh Pit as a lake and nature reserve.

=== co_corvane
title: Corvane Analytics (data company)
entities: Corvane Analytics, Juno Corvane, Quenhaven, Data Custody Law, Lisk Ridge data centre

Corvane Analytics is a Vallmoran technology company that provides data analysis and forecasting services to businesses and government agencies. It was founded in 2004 by the statistician Juno Corvane in a small office above a bakery in Quenhaven. The company is still headquartered in Quenhaven and employs about 2,100 people. Its clients include retailers, insurance companies, hospitals and several government ministries.

Corvane's flagship product is Tidewatch, a forecasting platform that predicts demand for goods and services by combining sales records, weather data and public statistics. The national health service uses Tidewatch to forecast hospital admissions during Hennow fever outbreak seasons, and Halvane Biologics has used it to plan Velorin production. The company also sells software for detecting fraud in insurance claims.

In 2021 the Data Custody Commissioner fined Corvane Analytics 18 million vell under the Data Custody Law after discovering that the company had stored customer records on servers outside Vallmora without authorisation. The records belonged to clients of an insurance company that used Corvane's fraud-detection service. Corvane did not appeal the fine and apologised publicly.

Following the fine, Corvane moved all of its data storage to the Lisk Ridge data centre, a new facility built on a hillside outside Quenhaven that opened in 2022. The data centre is cooled using water drawn from a deep aquifer, which the company says reduces its electricity use by about 40 percent compared with conventional cooling.

Corvane Analytics was listed on the Quenhaven Exchange in 2014. Juno Corvane remains chief executive and owns about 28 percent of the company's shares. In 2023 Corvane reported revenue of 610 million vell. The company sponsors the annual Corvane Prize for young statisticians, awarded by the University of Quenhaven.
