# Dynamic LoRA Switching/Routing and LLM Planning in Agentic AI Systems: A Technical Report

## TL;DR
- **Dynamic weight modification at inference falls into three architecturally distinct families that must not be conflated**: (a) *serving-level adapter hot-swapping* (S-LoRA, Punica, dLoRA, vLLM multi-LoRA) that batches thousands of independently-trained LoRAs on one base model with ~2ms/token overhead and up to 30× throughput over HF PEFT; (b) *routing/mixture over adapters* (X-LoRA, PHATGOOSE, Arrow, HydraLoRA, LoRAMoE) selecting/blending experts at token/layer/task granularity; and (c) *weight-generation and self-modification* (Text-to-LoRA, Drag-and-Drop LLMs, Transformer², SEAL) synthesizing or RL-editing weights on the fly.
- **LLM planning remains the binding constraint**: pure LLMs score near-random on obfuscated Blocksworld (~5%) and drop below 5% on 10-city NATURAL PLAN trip planning; the large reasoning model o1-preview reaches 97.8% on standard Blocksworld but only 52.8% on Mystery Blocksworld and degrades sharply beyond ~20 steps. The literature (Kambhampati's LLM-Modulo) converges on LLM-as-generator inside verifier-guided loops rather than LLM-as-planner.
- **The Part 1 × Part 2 intersection — a planner that emits adapter-selection actions — is largely open.** Zero-shot adapter routers (Arrow, PHATGOOSE) and skill libraries exist, but RL over adapter-selection policies, latency-aware routing under serving constraints, and verifiable skill composition are unfilled, tractable gaps in 2026.

## Key Findings

1. **Mergeability is the decisive property for hot-swapping.** Vanilla LoRA (W' = W₀ + (α/r)BA) merges into base weights for zero-latency single-adapter inference but must stay *unmerged* for multi-tenant batching. DoRA's per-column magnitude vector and normalization complicate clean additive cross-adapter merging; QLoRA's quantized base makes in-place merge lossy. This axis governs which serving strategy (merge vs unmerge vs dynamic) is optimal.
2. **dLoRA's central empirical insight** is that neither always-merged nor always-unmerged is optimal — it switches dynamically via a credit-based batching algorithm plus request-adapter co-migration, yielding up to 57.9× throughput over vLLM, 26.0× over HF PEFT, and up to 1.8× lower average latency than S-LoRA.
3. **Routing granularity is the key design axis** across the MoE-of-LoRAs family: token-level (X-LoRA, PHATGOOSE, MixLoRA), layer-level (MoLA), and task/sequence-level (LoRAHub, Arrow). Zero-shot routers (Arrow via SVD of adapter directions; PHATGOOSE via per-module gates) avoid retraining; trained routers (X-LoRA, LoRAMoE, HydraLoRA) achieve higher in-distribution accuracy.
4. **Weight generation is emerging as an alternative to routing entirely.** Text-to-LoRA (Sakana, ICML 2025) generates a task-specific adapter from a natural-language description in one forward pass; Drag-and-Drop LLMs generate LoRA weights zero-shot from unlabeled prompts with a claimed up to 12,000× lower overhead than full fine-tuning.
5. **Self-modification at test time works but is expensive.** Transformer² adapts via SVD-based z-vectors in a two-pass scheme; SEAL (MIT, NeurIPS 2025) has the model generate its own finetuning data via an RL loop, improving no-context SQuAD QA from 33.5%→47.0% and few-shot ARC from 20%→72.5%, but each self-edit evaluation costs 30–45s.

## Details

### PART 1 — Dynamic LoRA / Adapter Switching and Composition

#### 1.1 Foundations and variant mergeability

LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022, arXiv 2106.09685) freezes W₀ and learns a low-rank update:

  h = W₀x + ΔWx = W₀x + (α/r)·BAx,  B ∈ ℝ^(d×r), A ∈ ℝ^(r×k), r ≪ min(d,k)

The critical property for dynamic serving: because ΔW = (α/r)BA is a plain additive term, it can either be **merged** (W' = W₀ + (α/r)BA — zero inference overhead, one base copy per adapter) or kept **unmerged** (computed as a separate xAB branch — enables batching many adapters against one shared W₀). Every serving and routing system below exploits this choice.

Variant implications for hot-swapping:
- **QLoRA** (Dettmers et al., NeurIPS 2023): 4-bit NF4 quantized frozen base + LoRA. Cheap to store; merging into a quantized base is lossy, so it is typically served unmerged.
- **DoRA** (Liu et al., ICML 2024 Oral, arXiv 2402.09353, NVIDIA): decomposes W into magnitude m and direction, DoRA = m·(W₀+BA)/‖W₀+BA‖_c with column-wise norm ‖·‖_c. Adds no inference latency *for a single adapter* (m and BA fold into W after training), but the per-column magnitude vector and normalization make *cross-adapter additive merging* and clean unmerged multi-tenant batching harder than vanilla LoRA.
- **AdaLoRA** (Zhang et al., ICLR 2023): parameterizes ΔW = PΛQ in SVD form and adaptively prunes singular values to allocate rank by importance — variable effective rank per layer complicates fixed-rank batching kernels.
- **VeRA** (shared frozen random A,B + tiny trainable scaling vectors), **LoRA+** (different LRs for A vs B), **rsLoRA** (rank-stabilized scaling α/√r), **PiSSA** (initializes A,B from principal singular components of W₀ for faster convergence; MiLoRA uses minor components), **LoHa/LoKr** (Hadamard/Kronecker factorizations), **ReLoRA** (periodic merge-and-restart during training), **GaLore** (low-rank *gradient* projection, not a low-rank weight adapter — not an "adapter" to swap at all). Cheap-to-hotswap set: vanilla LoRA, VeRA, rsLoRA, LoRA+, PiSSA (all reduce to plain additive BA at inference). Complicating set: DoRA (magnitude vector), AdaLoRA (variable rank), LoHa/LoKr (non-additive product structure).

#### 1.2 Serving systems for multi-LoRA at scale

**Punica** (Chen et al., MLSys 2024, arXiv 2310.18547, UW/Duke): introduces the **SGMV (Segmented Gather Matrix-Vector multiplication)** CUDA kernel that batches the xAB computation for *different* LoRA models in one kernel launch, so the GPU holds a single copy of W₀. Reported **12× higher throughput** vs state-of-the-art (HF Transformers, DeepSpeed, FasterTransformer, vLLM naive) while adding only **~2ms latency per token**. The strong "batching effect" means full-batch latency is nearly identical to a single input.

**S-LoRA** (Sheng et al., MLSys 2024, arXiv 2311.03285, UC Berkeley/Stanford): stores *all* adapters in host memory and fetches active ones to GPU on demand. Three innovations: (1) **Unified Paging** — a single paged memory pool managing both variable-rank adapter weights and variable-length KV-cache tensors, generalizing vLLM's PagedAttention to reduce fragmentation; (2) **heterogeneous batching** via custom kernels **MBGMM** (Multi-size Batched Gather Matrix-Matrix, prefill, Triton) and **MBGMV** (decode, modified from Punica's BGMV to support non-contiguous memory and multiple ranks in a batch *without padding* — so cost scales with *average* rank not *max* rank); (3) a novel **S-LoRA tensor parallelism** minimizing added communication. Results: serves **thousands of adapters on a single GPU** (holds ~2000 adapters at near-constant throughput while baselines OOM), up to **30× throughput vs HF PEFT** and up to **4× vs vLLM** naive LoRA, evaluated on Llama-7B/13B/30B/70B. Max adapter count is bounded by host memory, not GPU memory.

**dLoRA** (Wu et al., OSDI 2024, PKU/Shanghai AI Lab): the key insight is that unmerged batching is not always beneficial — under skewed request distributions it is better to *merge* the dominant adapter. dLoRA dynamically (i) **merges/unmerges** adapters with the base model per replica via a **credit-based batching algorithm**, and (ii) **co-migrates requests and adapters** across worker replicas to fix autoregressive load imbalance. Verbatim result (evaluated on Llama-2 7B/13B/70B, ShareGPT trace, 500ms/token SLO): "dLoRA improves the throughput by up to 57.9× and 26.0×, compared to vLLM and HuggingFace PEFT, respectively. Compared to the concurrent work S-LoRA, dLoRA achieves up to 1.8× lower average latency." This dynamic merge/unmerge decision is the systems-level analogue of the routing question.

**Follow-on systems (2025–2026):** CaraServe (GPU-CPU cold-start-aware), Chameleon (adapter-aware non-preemptive multi-queue scheduler avoiding head-of-line blocking; caches adapters in GPU memory), Cannikin (SLO-tailored scheduling), InfiniLoRA (disaggregated multi-LoRA), V-LoRA (vision-LMM), a CUDA-stream-based *MixLoRA serving* system (parallel execution of *mixed-rank* requests — distinct from the MixLoRA training method in §1.3), and ExpertWeave (expert-specialized adapters at scale). These extend Punica/S-LoRA/dLoRA on scheduling, quantization co-design, and prefix-cache co-design.

**Production stacks:** vLLM multi-LoRA (`--enable-lora`, `--lora-modules name=path`; `max_loras` = max adapters per batch, `max_lora_rank` ∈ {8,16,32,…,512}, `max_cpu_loras` = LRU host cache and must be ≥ `max_loras`; adapters hot-swap via a queue; `fully_sharded_loras` and per-active-adapter CUDA-graph capture for variable usage patterns). LoRAX/Predibase, NVIDIA NIM multi-LoRA, TensorRT-LLM LoRA, and Ray Serve/Anyscale wrappers build on these. HuggingFace PEFT exposes the multi-adapter API: `add_adapter`, `set_adapter`, `disable_adapter`, and `add_weighted_adapter` with combination types `linear`, `cat`, `ties`, `dare_ties`, `dare_linear`, `magnitude_prune`, and SVD variants (`svd`, `ties_svd`, `dare_ties_svd`, `dare_linear_svd`).

#### 1.3 Routing / mixture over adapters ("MoE of LoRAs")

| Method | Routing granularity | Trained/zero-shot | Router parameterization | Reported benefit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LoRAHub** (Huang et al., COLM 2024, arXiv 2307.13269) | task/sequence (one coefficient vector w per task) | few-shot, gradient-free | CMA-ES black-box optimization (via Shiwa) of w over N adapters; objective L + α·Σ\|wᵢ\| | Matches few-shot ICL on BBH without gradients or extra params |
| **Arrow** (Ostapenko et al., ICML 2024, arXiv 2405.11157, Microsoft/Mila) | token + layer (top-3 modules/token/layer) | zero-shot | routes by \|dot product\| of activation with top right-singular vector (SVD) of each adapter's ΔW; paired with MBC (model-based clustering) library | Near-oracle routing on Phi-2/Mistral held-out tasks; matches/beats joint multitask training |
| **PHATGOOSE** (Muqeeth et al., ICML 2024, arXiv 2402.05859) | token + module | post-hoc (cheap per-expert gate training) | each contributor trains a sigmoid gate after PEFT; top-k routing by dot product of activation vs gate vectors | Outperforms prior post-hoc routing; sometimes beats explicit multitask training; ~matches oracle |
| **X-LoRA** (Buehler & Buehler, APL Machine Learning 2024, arXiv 2402.07148) | token + layer (dense or top-k) | trained | ~5M-param scaling head; dual forward pass (pass 1 → hidden states → scalings; pass 2 applies scalings) | Fine-grained never-before-used layer-wise combinations; strong on protein/materials tasks |
| **HydraLoRA** (Tian et al., NeurIPS 2024 Oral, arXiv 2404.19245) | intrinsic-component (sample) | trained | 1 shared A + multiple B experts; trainable MoE softmax router Wg∈ℝ^(r×N) | LLaMA2-7B MMLU 47.22 at 0.124% params vs LoRA(r=32) 46.59 at 0.248% (~2× fewer params); BBH 41.5 vs LoRA-MoE 40.3 at ~8.7× fewer params |
| **MixLoRA** (Li et al., arXiv 2404.15159) | token (top-k) | trained | LoRA experts in FFN + top-k router + separate attention LoRA + load-balance aux loss | ~9% accuracy over SOTA PEFT in multi-task; −40% GPU memory, −30% token latency |
| **LoRAMoE** (Dou et al., ACL 2024, arXiv 2312.09979) | token (per FFN layer) | trained | multiple LoRA experts per FFN + router; **localized balancing constraint** splitting experts into knowledge vs task groups; frozen backbone | up to 63.9% improvement on world-knowledge vs vanilla SFT (avoids catastrophic forgetting during 3M-sample instruction tuning) |
| **MoLA** (Gao et al.) | layer-wise expert allocation | trained | more experts in higher layers | Better parameter allocation than uniform |
| **MoLE / MoELoRA / SiRA / MoRAL / Omni-SMoLA / Mixture-of-Subspaces** | token/layer | trained | learned gates | Various in-distribution gains |
| **LoRA-Flow** (Wang et al., ACL 2024) | token, layer-wise dynamic fusion weights | trained gates | fusion gates for generation | Beats task-level LoRAHub weighting on generation |
| **Twin-Merging** (Lu et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2406.15479) | test-time, per-input | router selects shared + exclusive knowledge | dynamic merge of shared + task-exclusive components | Recovers most of the multi-task gap |
| **Self-MoE / MiXSE** (Kang et al., 2024) | token | self-specialized | model generates its own synthetic data to build experts + self-optimized router | Improves over base across domains without human labels |
| **LoRA-Switch, MeteoRA, DLP-LoRA** | token/sentence | trained | full-mode MoE / mini-MLP (5M params) fusion via top-p | DLP-LoRA keeps inference <2× single-LoRA |

Cross-cutting: task-level methods (LoRAHub, AdapterSoup) pick one blend per task; token-level methods (X-LoRA, PHATGOOSE, MixLoRA) re-route each token each layer. Zero-shot routers (Arrow, PHATGOOSE) are attractive for lifelong learning because no gradient is needed to add a new expert, but carry a "no free lunch" — routing quality depends on adapter directions being separable in activation/SVD space.

#### 1.4 Hypernetwork / weight-generation approaches

- **HyperNetworks** (Ha et al., ICLR 2017): a small network generates weights for a larger one — the conceptual root.
- **HyperFormer/HyperFormer++** (Karimi Mahabadi et al., ACL 2021), **Hypertuning** (Phang et al., 2022): hypernetworks generate adapter/prefix parameters conditioned on task.
- **Text-to-LoRA (T2L)** (Charakorn, Cetin, Tang & Lange, Sakana AI, ICML 2025, arXiv 2506.06105): a hypernetwork that, given a natural-language task description (embedded via the gte-large-en-v1.5 encoder), emits LoRA (A^l, B^l) per layer in "a single inexpensive forward pass." Trained on a suite of 9 pre-trained LoRA adapters (GSM8K, Arc-Challenge, BoolQ, etc.); reconstructed adapters "match the performance of task-specific adapters"; generalizes zero-shot to unseen tasks from descriptions; ~4× cheaper than 3-shot ICL. Uses module-specific and layer-specific embeddings with small per-layer MLPs. Limitation: still does not reach task-specific-LoRA benchmark performance in a zero-shot manner. Companion **Doc-to-LoRA (D2L)** (2026) internalizes long documents into weights.
- **Drag-and-Drop LLMs (DnD)** (Liang et al., arXiv 2506.16406, v1 19 Jun 2025, NUS-lineage): maps *unlabeled prompts* → LoRA weights via a lightweight text encoder + cascaded hyper-convolutional decoder, trained once on prompt–checkpoint pairs. Verbatim claim: "yielding i) up to 12,000× lower overhead than full fine-tuning, ii) average gains up to 30% in performance over the strongest training LoRAs on unseen common-sense reasoning, math, coding, and multimodal benchmarks" (self-reported, single 2025 preprint, "up to" qualifiers — not independently replicated).
- **Diffusion-based weight generation**: p-diff ("Neural Network Diffusion"), RPG (Recurrent Parameter Generation) — generate network parameters via diffusion; DnD builds on this lineage.
- **ICL vs weight-generation tradeoff**: ICL pays quadratic attention cost per query and needs the KV cache resident (>12GB for 128K tokens); weight generation amortizes this into a one-shot ≤50MB parameter update, but risks lossy compression and cannot condition on new in-context specifics unless re-generated.

#### 1.5 Self-modification / test-time weight adaptation

- **Transformer² (Transformer-squared)** (Sun/Cetin/Tang, Sakana AI, arXiv 2501.06252): **Singular Value Fine-tuning (SVF)** learns a vector z∈ℝ^r per weight matrix that scales singular values: W' = UΣ'Vᵀ, Σ' = Σ⊗diag(z). z-vectors are trained per skill with RL. At inference, a **two-pass** scheme: pass 1 identifies the task (prompt-based, classifier-based, or few-shot mixture), pass 2 composes the relevant z-vectors and runs the query. Claims to outperform LoRA with far fewer parameters, with inherent compositionality.
- **SEAL (Self-Adapting LLMs)** (Zweiger & Pari et al., MIT, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2506.10943): nested-loop meta-learning — an **outer RL loop** (ReST^EM / "SFT on good self-edits") optimizes generation of "self-edits" (synthetic finetuning data + hyperparameters); an **inner loop** applies them via LoRA SFT for persistent weight updates. Verbatim results: "following reinforcement learning (RL) training, finetuning on self-generated synthetic data improves question-answering performance on the no-passage-in-context variant of SQuAD from 33.5% to 47.0%" (Qwen2.5-7B; beats GPT-4.1-generated data), continued-pretraining 200-passage setting 43.8%, and few-shot ARC subset **72.5%** vs 20% (self-edits without RL) vs 0% (no adaptation) on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct. Costs: each self-edit eval takes 30–45s; RL mitigates catastrophic forgetting better than plain SFT (per co-author).
- **Test-Time Training (TTT)**: TTT layers (Sun et al.) make the hidden state itself a model updated by a self-supervised loss at test time; **test-time training on in-context examples for ARC** (Akyürek et al., 2024) gave large ARC gains by LoRA-tuning on augmented demos per task.
- **Titans / Neural Memory** (Behrouz et al., Google, 2024–2025): a neural long-term memory module updated at test time via a **surprise** signal (gradient of the memory loss), with momentum and forget gating — "learning to memorize at test time."
- **Fast weights / linear-attention-as-fast-weights** (Schlag, Irie & Schmidhuber, ICML 2021): linear Transformers are formally fast-weight programmers; Hebbian-style outer-product updates are the historical root of test-time weight change. **Dynamic evaluation** (Krause et al.) is the classic LM analogue.

#### 1.6 Architecture-level dynamism beyond LoRA (the taxonomy that matters)

The user's requested distinction, made explicit:
- **Routing between models** (whole separate checkpoints): **RouteLLM** (Ong et al., arXiv 2406.18665, ICLR 2025) — trains routers (incl. a matrix-factorization router) on Chatbot-Arena preference data to route between a strong model (GPT-4-1106-preview, ≈$24.7/M tokens) and a weak model (Mixtral-8×7B-instruct, ≈$0.24/M tokens), achieving "cost savings of up to 3.66×" while retaining 95% GPT-4 quality on MT-Bench. FrugalGPT (Chen et al., TMLR 2024) — cascades, up to 98% cost reduction; Hybrid-LLM, Zooter, RouterBench (Hu et al., 2024 — 405K outcomes, 11 models), RouterArena, SPROUT (45K queries, 14 models); commercial NotDiamond (<60ms decisions), Martian. Router overhead: rule-based <1ms, embedding ~5ms, ML classifier 50–100ms.
- **Routing between experts inside one model** (sub-layer FFN experts, jointly trained): Switch Transformer (Fedus et al.), Mixtral 8×7B, **DeepSeek-V3** (671B total / 37B active; 1 shared + 256 routed experts, 8 activated; **auxiliary-loss-free load balancing** via per-expert bias bᵢ added only to the top-k selection score sᵢ+bᵢ (sᵢ = hᵀeᵢ) and updated by γ=0.001 per step, not to the gating weight — avoids routing collapse without the gradient interference of aux losses; a tiny complementary sequence-wise balance loss is retained), Qwen3-MoE. Also **Mixture-of-Depths** (dynamic per-token layer skipping via capacity-based routing) and **Mixture-of-Recursions**.
- **Routing between adapters on one base model** (the report's core): everything in §1.2–1.3.

Related dynamic-compute mechanisms: early-exit / layer-skipping (CALM, LayerSkip, SkipDecode, ShortGPT, LayerDrop), speculative decoding (dynamic compute allocation), and **model merging at inference** — task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023: task vectors τ = θ_SFT − θ_PRE), **TIES-merging** (Yadav et al., NeurIPS 2023: Trim → Elect-Sign → Disjoint-Merge to resolve sign conflicts), **DARE** (Yu et al., ICML 2024: randomly drop ~90% of delta params and rescale by 1/density; a plug-in before TIES/task-arithmetic), model soups, and **Evolutionary Model Merge** (Sakana). Caveat from a 2025 systematic study: on LLMs, only Task Arithmetic consistently gives constructive gains (typically <1% avg); TIES can even degrade; DARE fails when merging many models.

### PART 2 — LLM Planning Capabilities

#### 2.1 Empirical limits

- **PlanBench** (Valmeekam et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B, arXiv 2206.10498): Blocksworld + Mystery Blocksworld (syntactic obfuscation). Classical Fast Downward = 100%. On 600 Blocksworld instances: LLaMA-3 405B 62.6% (zero-shot) / 47.3% (one-shot), GPT-4 34.6%, GPT-4o 35.5%; on Mystery Blocksworld the best pre-o1 LLMs barely exceed ~5%.
- **LRMs move the needle but don't saturate** (Valmeekam/Stechly/Kambhampati, "LLMs Still Can't Plan; Can LRMs?", arXiv 2409.13373): o1-preview **97.8%** on standard Blocksworld but **52.8%** on Mystery Blocksworld; degrades sharply beyond ~20 steps; cannot reliably detect unsolvable instances; raises cost/guarantee concerns.
- **NATURAL PLAN** (Zheng et al., Google, arXiv 2406.04520): Trip/Meeting/Calendar in natural language with tool outputs as context. Trip Planning solve rates: Gemini 1.5 Pro 34.8%, GPT-4 31.1%, GPT-3.5 7.3%, GPT-4o 3.7%. Verbatim: "all models perform below 5% when there are 10 cities, highlighting a significant gap in planning in natural language for SoTA LLMs."
- Other harnesses: **ACPBench**, **AutoPlanBench**, **TravelPlanner** (sandbox with tools), **AgentBench**, **τ-bench/τ²-bench** (tool-agent-user), **WebArena/VisualWebArena**, **OSWorld**, **GAIA**, **SWE-bench Verified**, **BFCL** (tool/function-call routing).
- **Core diagnosis** (Kambhampati et al., "LLMs Can't Plan, But Can Help Planning in LLM-Modulo Frameworks", ICML 2024): autoregressive generation does approximate retrieval, not sound state-tracking; valid long-horizon plans need consistent world-state maintenance and backtracking.

#### 2.2 Planning algorithms and scaffolds

- **Prompting/agent scaffolds**: ReAct (reason+act interleaving), Reflexion (verbal self-reflection memory), Chain/Tree/Graph-of-Thought, Least-to-Most, Plan-and-Solve, ADaPT (as-needed recursive decomposition).
- **Symbolic hybrids**: **LLM+P** (Liu et al., 2023 — LLM translates NL → PDDL, hands to a classical planner; first to give optimal plans where LLMs fail on most instances), LLM+DP, and **LLM-Modulo** (Kambhampati et al., ICML 2024 — LLM generator inside a loop with sound external verifiers/critics; performance bounded by verifier coverage).
- **Search over language**: **RAP/Reasoning-via-Planning** (Hao et al., EMNLP 2023 — MCTS with the LLM as world model), **LATS** (Zhou et al., ICML 2024, arXiv 2310.04406 — MCTS unifying reasoning+acting+planning with the LLM as agent, value fn, and self-reflection; 94.4% pass@1 on HumanEval with GPT-4 and 75.9 on WebShop; uses on average 3.55 fewer expanded nodes than RAP and 12.12 fewer than ToT), ToT+MCTS, AlphaZero-like search.
- **Test-time compute scaling**: Snell et al. (arXiv 2408.03314, ICLR 2025) — two mechanisms (search against dense **PRM** verifiers, and adaptive updates of the proposal distribution); a "compute-optimal" per-prompt allocation is >4× more efficient than best-of-N, and under FLOPs-matched eval a small model can beat a 14× larger one on problems where it has non-trivial success. **PRM vs ORM**: process reward models score intermediate steps (better for search); outcome reward models score final answers. o1/o3 internalize RL+search.
- Others: self-consistency, verifier-guided beam search, world-models for planning, HTN-with-LLMs, Voyager (skill library for Minecraft), AutoGPT-class failure modes (goal drift, no backtracking), agentic memory systems.

#### 2.3 Routing + planning intersection

- **Tool/API routing**: Toolformer (self-supervised API-call insertion), Gorilla (API-call generation), ToolLLM/RestGPT, function-calling routers benchmarked by BFCL.
- **Orchestration primitives**: LangGraph (explicit state-graph control flow), AutoGen (conversational multi-agent), CrewAI (role-based), OpenAI Swarm/Agents SDK (handoffs), Anthropic MCP (tool/context protocol), Semantic Router (embedding-similarity intent routing). Algorithmic core = classify-then-dispatch (cost-aware, sometimes cascade), semantic caching for repeat queries, and multi-agent handoff patterns.

### PART 3 — Synthesis for a Researcher

#### 3.1 Adapter/expert routing AS a planning mechanism

The literature already treats **skills as adapters**: Arrow + MBC build and reuse a *library of LoRAs* with zero-shot routing; PHATGOOSE recycles an "ocean of experts"; Self-MoE builds self-specialized experts; LoRAHub composes skills per new task. This is the modular-deep-learning vision (Pfeiffer et al., "Modular Deep Learning" survey, 2023; AdapterFusion, Pfeiffer et al., EACL 2021 — two-stage non-destructive adapter composition). **Continual/lifelong learning** benefits because zero-shot routers add experts without gradient updates (Arrow's router is local to each expert, so it is less susceptible to catastrophic forgetting); LoRAMoE explicitly prevents world-knowledge forgetting during instruction tuning via its localized balancing constraint (up to 63.9% over vanilla SFT); Lifelong-MoE grows experts over task streams.

**Known negative results / limitations** (critical for the researcher):
- **Routing collapse / load imbalance**: MoE routers collapse to a few experts without balancing; DeepSeek-V3's aux-loss-free bias is the current best mitigation, but a 2025 study finds it insufficient alone under super-high sparsity in lower layers.
- **Cross-adapter merge degradation**: TIES/DARE can *degrade* LLM performance; only task arithmetic reliably gives (small, <1%) constructive gains; DARE fails when merging many models; rank/sign interference between adapters is real.
- **Zero-shot routing "no free lunch"**: Arrow/PHATGOOSE depend on adapter directions being separable; on MBC libraries "routing is less important," meaning the gain comes partly from clustering, not routing per se.
- **DoRA/variable-rank adapters** break the clean batching assumptions of the Punica/S-LoRA kernels.

#### 3.2 Open gaps & promising 2026 directions

1. **Learned planners that emit adapter-selection actions.** No mature system treats "load LoRA k" as an action in a planning/RL policy. Combining LATS-style search with an action space that includes *adapter switches* (skills) is unexplored and tractable.
2. **RL over adapter-selection policies.** SEAL and Transformer² hint at this (SEAL RL-optimizes self-edits; Transformer² RL-trains z-vectors) but neither *plans* over a discrete skill library with verifier feedback.
3. **Latency-aware routing under serving constraints.** Integrate the router's expert choice with dLoRA/S-LoRA merge/unmerge and cache-eviction decisions — jointly optimize accuracy and SLO. Routing (§1.3) and serving (§1.2) are currently studied separately.
4. **Verifiable skill composition.** Apply LLM-Modulo-style external verifiers to *which adapter to trust*, closing the loop between symbolic verification and neural routing.
5. **Missing benchmarks.** There is no PlanBench-analogue for "skill/adapter selection as planning" — a benchmark measuring whether a router+library can *plan a sequence of skill invocations* to solve a compositional task, with plan-length and obfuscation axes like PlanBench.

#### 3.3 Practical implementation stack (what to build today)

- **Serving/routing experiments**: vLLM (`--enable-lora`; tune `max_loras`, `max_lora_rank`, `max_cpu_loras`) as the batching backbone; LoRAX or a dLoRA-style merge/unmerge controller for skewed workloads; HF PEFT for `add_weighted_adapter` composition (linear/ties/dare) and X-LoRA. For token-level mixture research, X-LoRA's PEFT integration (dual forward pass ⇒ 2 KV caches — a real cost) or Arrow/PHATGOOSE implementations.
- **Weight-generation experiments**: Text-to-LoRA and SEAL public code (SEAL runs on 2× A100/H100).
- **Planning harnesses**: PlanBench (karthikv792/LLMs-Planning), NATURAL PLAN (google-deepmind/natural-plan), LM-Eval-Harness, LATS repo.
- **Hardware**: a single A100/H100 (80GB) suffices for multi-LoRA serving of thousands of small-rank adapters (S-LoRA) and for SEAL-scale RL; token-level MoE-LoRA training (MixLoRA) is explicitly designed for <24GB consumer GPUs.
- **Real engineering bottlenecks**: (1) kernel-level batching of *heterogeneous ranks* (SGMV/BGMV pad to max rank; S-LoRA's MBGMV avoids padding — pick accordingly); (2) adapter cache eviction (vLLM LRU over `max_cpu_loras`; mitigate cold-start by pre-loading with a dummy request); (3) merge/unmerge overhead (dLoRA's credit-based decision); (4) X-LoRA-style dual forward pass doubling the KV cache; (5) DoRA/AdaLoRA breaking fixed-rank kernels.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 (weeks 1–4): establish the substrate.** Stand up vLLM multi-LoRA serving with 50–2000 small-rank (r=8–16) adapters on one A100/H100; reproduce S-LoRA-style throughput vs HF PEFT. In parallel, run PlanBench + NATURAL PLAN on your target base model to fix a planning baseline. *Threshold to proceed:* you can hot-swap adapters at <10ms and reproduce the ~5% Mystery-Blocksworld / <5% 10-city NATURAL-PLAN failures, giving you a measurable planning gap.

**Stage 2 (weeks 5–10): build the skill library + router.** Train 10–50 task LoRAs; implement Arrow (zero-shot SVD routing) and PHATGOOSE (per-expert gate) as baselines; compare against LoRAHub (CMA-ES) and a trained X-LoRA/HydraLoRA router. Measure both accuracy and routing entropy/load balance. *Change decision:* if zero-shot routing matches trained routing on held-out tasks, prioritize lifelong-learning experiments (adding experts without retraining); if not, invest in learned routers with DeepSeek-V3-style aux-loss-free balancing.

**Stage 3 (weeks 11+): the novel contribution — planning over the skill library.** Frame adapter selection as actions in a LATS/MCTS search or an RL policy with an external verifier (LLM-Modulo). Reward = verifier-confirmed subtask success. *Benchmarks that would change the thesis:* if a fixed merged model (task arithmetic) matches your dynamic router on the compositional task, the dynamism isn't buying anything — pivot to harder compositional/obfuscated splits. If DnD/Text-to-LoRA zero-shot generation matches library-routing, pivot from routing to generation.

**Cost-aware default:** for any deployment, put a RouteLLM/cascade layer *in front of* the adapter system — most traffic won't need the specialized skill, and the router adds <100ms.

## Caveats

- **"Up to" figures are best-case, self-reported.** dLoRA's 57.9×, S-LoRA's 30×/4×, Punica's 12×, RouteLLM's 3.66×, MixLoRA's 9%/−40%/−30%, HydraLoRA's gains, and especially DnD's 12,000×/30% are single-paper best-case numbers under specific workloads/skews. Real gains depend heavily on request distribution (skewed vs distinct adapters) and rank heterogeneity — dLoRA itself shows the optimal strategy *changes with skew*.
- **The Sakana Doc-to-LoRA/T2L 2026 latency claims** (<1s, <50MB) and some 2026 serving-system numbers come from vendor posts / very recent preprints not yet independently replicated; treat as directional.
- **Planning numbers move fast**: o1/o3 results are on static PlanBench splits; leakage and prompt-format sensitivity are ongoing concerns, and Mystery Blocksworld specifically controls for memorization — trust the obfuscated numbers over the standard ones.
- **Routing ≠ planning.** Most "routing" work (RouteLLM, Arrow, PHATGOOSE) is single-shot classification, not sequential planning. The synthesis in Part 3 is a research proposal, not established practice — the intersection is genuinely under-explored, which is the opportunity.
- Several methods requested (MoRAL, Omni-SMoLA, LoRA Soups, Mixture-of-Subspaces, MoLA specifics) are covered at summary depth only due to search-budget limits; verify their exact reported numbers against the primary arXiv PDFs before citing.
